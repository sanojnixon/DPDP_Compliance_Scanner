# Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — Section Reference

> Extracted from the DPDP Act (No. 22 of 2023), dated 11th August 2023.
> This file is used by the AI reasoning layer to identify which sections of the Act
> are violated when scanning banking app screenshots.

## Chapter I — Preliminary

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 1 | Short title and commencement | Title of the Act and commencement provisions |
| Section 2 | Definitions | Definitions of key terms: Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, Data Processor, personal data, processing, consent, etc. |
| Section 3 | Application of Act | Territorial scope — applies to digital personal data processing within India and extraterritorial processing connected to offering goods/services in India |

## Chapter II — Obligations of Data Fiduciary

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 4 | Grounds for processing personal data | Personal data may only be processed for lawful purposes with consent or for certain legitimate uses |
| Section 5 | Notice | Data Fiduciary must give notice to Data Principal before or with consent request, specifying: (i) personal data and purpose, (ii) how to exercise rights, (iii) how to complain to Board |
| Section 5(1) | Notice with consent request | Every consent request must be accompanied/preceded by a notice informing the Data Principal |
| Section 5(1)(i) | Purpose in notice | Notice must specify personal data and purpose of processing |
| Section 5(1)(ii) | Rights in notice | Notice must inform Data Principal how to exercise rights under Section 6(4) and Section 13 |
| Section 5(1)(iii) | Complaint mechanism in notice | Notice must inform how to complain to the Board |
| Section 5(2) | Pre-existing consent notice | If consent was given before Act commencement, Data Fiduciary must give notice as soon as practicable |
| Section 5(3) | Language of notice | Notice must be available in English or any Eighth Schedule language |
| Section 6 | Consent | Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, with clear affirmative action, limited to necessary data for specified purpose |
| Section 6(1) | Consent requirements | Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous; limited to data necessary for specified purpose |
| Section 6(2) | Invalid consent | Any part of consent infringing the Act is invalid to the extent of infringement |
| Section 6(3) | Consent language and contact | Consent requests must be in clear, plain language with DPO contact details |
| Section 6(4) | Right to withdraw consent | Data Principal may withdraw consent at any time, with ease comparable to giving consent |
| Section 6(5) | Consequences of withdrawal | Consequences of withdrawal borne by Data Principal; does not affect legality of prior processing |
| Section 6(6) | Cessation on withdrawal | Data Fiduciary must cease processing after consent withdrawal (unless authorised by law) |
| Section 6(7) | Consent Manager | Data Principal may manage consent through a Consent Manager |
| Section 7 | Certain legitimate uses | Legitimate uses without explicit consent: voluntary provision, State functions, legal obligations, medical emergencies, employment, etc. |
| Section 8 | General obligations of Data Fiduciary | Comprehensive obligations covering compliance responsibility, data processors, data accuracy, security, breach notification, retention, grievance redressal |
| Section 8(1) | Compliance responsibility | Data Fiduciary responsible for compliance irrespective of agreements or Data Principal's failure to perform duties |
| Section 8(2) | Data Processor engagement | Data Processor may only be engaged under valid contract |
| Section 8(3) | Data accuracy | Data Fiduciary must ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency when data is used for decisions or shared with another Data Fiduciary |
| Section 8(4) | Technical and organisational measures | Data Fiduciary must implement appropriate measures for effective compliance |
| Section 8(5) | Security safeguards | Data Fiduciary must protect personal data with reasonable security safeguards to prevent data breach |
| Section 8(6) | Breach notification | Data Fiduciary must notify the Board and affected Data Principals of any personal data breach |
| Section 8(7) | Data retention and erasure | Data must be erased when consent is withdrawn or specified purpose is no longer served, unless retention is required by law |
| Section 8(8) | Purpose deemed no longer served | Purpose is deemed no longer served if Data Principal has not approached the Data Fiduciary for the prescribed time period |
| Section 8(9) | DPO contact publication | Data Fiduciary must publish DPO business contact information |
| Section 8(10) | Grievance redressal mechanism | Data Fiduciary must establish an effective grievance redressal mechanism |
| Section 9 | Processing of personal data of children | Special protections for children's data including verifiable parental consent, no detrimental processing, no tracking/behavioural monitoring/targeted advertising |
| Section 9(1) | Verifiable parental consent | Must obtain verifiable consent of parent/guardian before processing child's data |
| Section 9(2) | No detrimental processing | Must not process data likely to cause detrimental effect on child's well-being |
| Section 9(3) | No tracking or targeting children | Must not track, behaviourally monitor, or target advertising at children |
| Section 10 | Additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary | Must appoint DPO, independent data auditor, conduct periodic DPIA and audits |

## Chapter III — Rights and Duties of Data Principal

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 11 | Right to access information about personal data | Data Principal can request summary of data processed, identities of all processors/fiduciaries it was shared with |
| Section 12 | Right to correction and erasure of personal data | Right to request correction, completion, updating, and erasure of personal data |
| Section 12(2) | Correction obligations | Data Fiduciary must correct inaccurate, complete incomplete, and update personal data upon request |
| Section 12(3) | Erasure obligations | Data Fiduciary must erase personal data upon request unless retention is necessary |
| Section 13 | Right of grievance redressal | Data Principal has right to grievance redressal from Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager; must exhaust this before approaching Board |
| Section 14 | Right to nominate | Data Principal may nominate another individual to exercise rights in case of death or incapacity |
| Section 15 | Duties of Data Principal | Duties: comply with laws, no impersonation, no suppression of material info, no false complaints, furnish authentic info |

## Chapter IV — Special Provisions

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 16 | Processing of personal data outside India | Central Government may restrict transfer of personal data to certain countries |
| Section 17 | Exemptions | Exemptions for legal proceedings, law enforcement, research, State functions; State processing exempted from certain provisions |

## Chapter V — Data Protection Board of India

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 18 | Establishment of Board | Establishes Data Protection Board of India |
| Section 19 | Composition and qualifications | Board shall have Chairperson and Members with relevant expertise |

## Chapter VI — Powers, Functions and Procedure

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 27 | Powers and functions of Board | Board can inquire into breaches, impose penalties; acts on intimation, complaints, or government reference |
| Section 28 | Procedure to be followed by Board | Board functions as digital office; has powers of civil court |

## Chapter VII — Appeal and Alternate Dispute Resolution

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 29 | Appeal to Appellate Tribunal | Aggrieved person may appeal Board orders within 60 days |
| Section 31 | Alternate dispute resolution | Board may direct parties to mediation |

## Chapter VIII — Penalties and Adjudication

| Section | Title | Summary |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Section 33 | Penalties | Board may impose monetary penalties per the Schedule for significant breaches |

## Schedule — Penalty Amounts

| Breach | Penalty |
|--------|---------|
| Failure to take reasonable security safeguards (Section 8(5)) | Up to ₹250 crore |
| Failure to notify Board/Data Principal of breach (Section 8(6)) | Up to ₹200 crore |
| Breach of obligations regarding children (Section 9) | Up to ₹200 crore |
| Breach of Significant Data Fiduciary obligations (Section 10) | Up to ₹150 crore |
| Breach of Data Principal duties (Section 15) | Up to ₹10,000 |
| Breach of voluntary undertaking (Section 32) | Up to applicable limit |
| Any other breach of Act or rules | Up to ₹50 crore |

## Key Sections Relevant to Banking App Screen Compliance

The following sections are most frequently violated in banking app screenshots:

1. **Section 4** — Grounds for processing: data processed without lawful purpose
2. **Section 5(1)** — Notice: no notice accompanying consent request
3. **Section 5(1)(i)** — Purpose not specified in notice
4. **Section 5(1)(ii)** — Rights not communicated in notice
5. **Section 6(1)** — Consent: data collection exceeding what's necessary for stated purpose (data minimisation)
6. **Section 6(4)** — No mechanism to withdraw consent visible
7. **Section 8(3)** — Data accuracy: conflicting data values on same screen
8. **Section 8(5)** — Security safeguards: passwords, OTPs, credentials visible in plaintext
9. **Section 8(7)** — Data retention: personal data retained beyond purpose
10. **Section 8(9)** — DPO contact not published
11. **Section 8(10)** — Grievance redressal: no DPO/grievance mechanism visible on PII-heavy screens
12. **Section 9(1)** — Children: no guardian consent for minor accounts
13. **Section 9(3)** — Children: tracking or targeted advertising on child's account
